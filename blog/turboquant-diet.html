Google Just Put AI on a Diet. Your Turn.

September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

This week Google announced TurboQuant, a technique that shrinks the memory needed to run large language models by up to six times — without sacrificing accuracy. It works by optimizing how models store and reuse previous computations (the "key-value cache," for the nerds).

Wall Street noticed. Memory chipmakers shed nearly $100 billion in market value in days — SK Hynix down 6%, Samsung down 5%, Micron and SanDisk sliding behind them. Cloudflare's CEO called it "Google's DeepSeek," a nod to the Chinese lab that keeps humiliating billion-dollar models with leaner ones. DeepSeek itself just dropped a model priced at a fraction of a cent per million tokens, and enterprises are defecting from OpenAI over it.

The entire industry is suddenly obsessed with trimming the fat: fewer tokens, less memory, leaner models, lower bills. Anthropic's new Claude Opus 5.5 — announced literally yesterday — runs 40% cheaper than its predecessor.

Funny, isn't it? We'll ruthlessly optimize a neural network's waistline but treat our own bodies like legacy systems nobody's allowed to touch.

Here's the thing the AI industry understands that diet culture doesn't: willpower is a terrible optimization strategy. Systems beat motivation. Google didn't ask its models to try harder — it re-engineered the underlying mechanism. Modern prescription weight-loss programs work the same way: they don't lecture you about discipline, they change the biological cache settings — appetite signaling, metabolic efficiency, the stuff actually driving the outcome.
